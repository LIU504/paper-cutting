From Paper to Screen:
The Digital Preservation of Chinese Paper cutting



A comprehensive digital repository dedicated to documenting the artisanal heritage and cultural significance of jianzhi art during the lunar new year.

About this Website



Why fu and zodiac paper-cuts
In 2009 UNESCO inscribed "Chinese paper-cut" on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Its entry says paper-cuts are used to decorate windows, beds and ceilings and to mark weddings, birthdays and other celebrations (UNESCO, 2009). Pasting window flowers at Spring Festival is one of these uses; one craftsperson calls it "not just a form, but a culture" (China.org.cn, 2018). The fu character and the zodiac animals are both common Spring Festival subjects, their patterns are easy to recognise, and a lot of online material exists on them. That is why we chose them.

Scope of the background sources
UNESCO's entry covers Chinese paper-cutting as a whole, not just Spring Festival paper-cuts, so we use it only for background. Our analysis of Spring Festival paper-cuts rests on the fu and zodiac works and the cases we collected.

How we selected sources
We preferred sources with a named institution or author, works whose origin can be traced, and works whose source explains their use or meaning. Every claim about meaning is attributed to a source. Our own observations are kept separate.

Why we include works, making materials and cross-regional cases
A collection of finished works does not show how they are made, and a collection of making processes does not show how the paper-cuts are presented to different readers. We need all three to answer what is preserved and what changes when it is shared.

How a digital archive helps preserve culture
UNESCO notes that paper-cutting is mostly passed on within families, often from mother to daughter, starting in childhood (UNESCO, 2009), and one craftsperson says "many people no longer cut" (China.org.cn, 2018). Much of this knowledge lives in people's hands, and an archive cannot replace learning to cut by hand. It can do three smaller things. These are our own judgements, not conclusions from the sources.
- It keeps a work, its occasion of use, its meaning and its source in one record. Window flowers are festival decorations, so the pieces themselves may not last, but a record can last and can be checked.
- It shows what existing sources record and what they leave out. Both fu reports we collected describe the occasion and the meaning, and neither explains the pattern or the cutting method.
- It puts material from different regions and media into one format, which makes comparison easier.

What value the website may offer
Once built, the website may be useful to three groups. These are possible uses only. We have not tested the site with real users.
- Students and teachers learning about Spring Festival or paper-cutting can find sourced works and see what each source actually says.
- Curious general readers, including those unfamiliar with Chinese customs, can see fu and zodiac paper-cuts and how different regions describe them in one place.
- People building similar archives or doing similar research can reuse or compare our selection method, our archive card format and our source list.

Limitations
Our sample is small and does not represent all paper-cuts or all regions.

Archive of works

Paper Cutting of the Character Fu

Artwork Name: "Blessings Arrive Immediately" (Ma Shang You Fu)

Artwork description: This paper-cut is created by inheritor Cao Jing. The central element is the Chinese character "Fu" inside a round frame. A horse, lotus and koi fish are carved around the fu character. The horse symbolises good fortune coming soon, koi fish represent surplus and wealth. The symmetrical layout is a classic style of Shaanxi folk paper-cut, carrying people’s wishes for happiness and prosperity.

Source information:
- Artist: Cao Jing (曹静)
- Origin: Shaanxi, China
- Publishing institution and image source: Weinan Culture and Tourism Bureau
- Date: January 27, 2026
- In-text citation: Weinan Culture and Tourism Bureau, 2026

Artwork Name: "Fu Gui Fu" (Prosperity Fu)

Artwork description: This paper-cut is created by inheritor Zhou Dongmei. The work centers on the Chinese character "Fu", decorated with koi fish and peony flowers. The peony symbolises wealth and honour, while koi fish represent surplus and good fortune. It adopts the unique Pucheng paper-cut style of "painting within characters", conveying people’s wishes for wealth and a prosperous life.

Source information: - Artist: Zhou Dongmei (周冬梅)
- Date: December 4, 2023
- In-text citation: Southeast Net, 2023

Artwork Name: "Fu Yun Xiang Cheng" (Blessings and Auspiciousness)

Artwork description: This paper-cut is created by inheritor Chen Yimin. The work centers on the Chinese character "Fu". The strokes of the fu character are integrated with traditional Fujian landmarks, including ancient pagodas and tulou buildings. It adopts the delicate style of Zhangpu paper-cut from Fujian. The work combines local cultural features with the fu culture, conveying people’s wishes for good fortune and a happy life.

Source information: - Artist: Chen Yimin (陈艺敏)
- Origin: Zhangpu, Fujian, China
- Date: June 30, 2026
- In-text citation: China News Service, 2026

Chinese Zodiac Paper Cutting

Artwork Name: Rabbit — Mao Tu Zhao Feng Nian (《卯兔兆丰年》)

Artwork description: Created for the Year of the Rabbit, this paper-cut shows rabbits celebrating a harvest. Zheng Feiyan explains that the rabbit holding wheat ears represents a good harvest year after year.

Source information:
- Artist and image creator: Zheng Feiyan (郑飞雁)
- Origin: Dingbian, Shaanxi
- Publishing source: Cultural Arts News (文化艺术报), published on Sohu
- Page date: December 4, 2022
- Original page and image: https://www.sohu.com/a/613513612_556618
- In-text citation: (Zheng, 2022)

Artwork Name: Sheep — San Yang Kai Tai (《三羊开泰》)

Artwork description: The design uses sheep and 24 blades of grass. The artwork page explains that the grass represents a wish for sufficient food and clothing throughout the year’s 24 solar terms.

Source information:
- Artist: Zhang Xiaolin (张晓林)
- Origin: Beijing
- Publishing institution and image source: Beijing Jianwujie Culture Co., Ltd. (北京剪无界文化有限公司)
- Page date: February 26, 2020
- Original artwork page and image: https://www.jianwujie.com/projects/sykt.html

- In-text citation for the artwork page: (Beijing Jianwujie Culture Co., Ltd., 2020)

Artwork Name: Snake and rabbit — She Pan Tu (《蛇盘兔》)

Artwork description: Zhang Jie combines a snake and a rabbit, drawing on the folk saying “蛇盘兔，必定富” to express wishes for wealth and good fortune.

Source information:

Making and Digital Documentation

Photo :Shows the finished design and its details. The description can explain its meaning, use, and source.
Illustrated tutorial:Pairs pictures with instructions, making it easy to find and review each step.
Video tutorial:Shows hand movements and the cutting process continuously. If it includes narration, it can explain the steps and techniques in more detail.
